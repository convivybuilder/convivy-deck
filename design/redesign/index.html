Five screens, each in both themes, with a note on what changed from today and why. Read this page first; the others assume it.

The thesis

Deck is an instrument panel around a document. The native chrome (rail, session row, operator row, ribbon, cockpit) is flat, on hairlines, in one type family; the conversation, the one web pane in the window, is a document, which is how session_view.py already draws it. The redesign's job is the instrument panel.

One accent for in-progress marks, and it is the crew's hue. The code already rules that "shape carries identity, not colour alone" (CockpitView.RowTreatment) and that a rail row's dot is its crew's theme accent (RailRow). The redesign drops Color.accentColor, the system blue, from every in-progress mark (the half-filled glyph, the pull-request glyph, the current waypoint, Send) and gives them the selected crew's hue instead. Everything else keeps the colour it has: the rail's ten identity hues, the summons orange, the amber caution, red for errors, green for a pass. This is the one sentence the earlier draft's "one colour" overstated, and it is the sentence the mocks draw.

Density with clarity. The cockpit's body text moves from frameFont(.label) to frameFont(.body) — 10pt to 11pt at the default base, and regular weight rather than semibold, since .label was drawing every row title bold — rows get a fixed 14pt glyph column and a right-aligned monospace meta column, and the centre column's three chrome rows above the conversation become one.

The aesthetic risk

The frame takes the selected crew's hue. Today the conversation canvas is tinted with the crew's theme (canvasTint) and everything around it is system grey. In the mocks the chrome, the rail and the input surfaces carry the same hue at a tenth of the canvas's chroma (0.004–0.006 against 0.018/0.028), so selecting Vivy makes the whole window faintly green and selecting Deck makes it faintly blue. The risk is that ten differently coloured windows read as ten skins rather than one product, and that the tint is either invisible (and so pointless) or visible (and so garish). The cost is concrete: RailRow.markBacking, SessionPaneView.inkSurface and the selection wash all hardcode the surface they solve against, and each must take the tinted surface instead, or the solver and the view disagree exactly the way markBacking's doc records they once did. The build here solves every ink against the tinted surface for all ten hues, so the numbers below are the numbers the app would get.

Conceded from the first draft: the serif transcript was presented as a risk and it already ships. session_view.py sets the body to ui-serif, "New York", Georgia at 1.05rem/1.65 with the same rationale, the operator's own turns are serif behind a 3px rule (dashed at 55% when held), and tool calls are collapsed <details> rows in mono with no result column. The mocks now draw all of that as it ships, at SessionTypography's 15.75/1.65, and drop the web font and the invented diff stats.

Three inks, three jobs. --ink is primary at 7:1 (the foreground recipe). --ink-2 is every secondary thing a person reads, at 4.5: meta columns, timestamps, section labels, "18 more". --ink-3 is solved to 3.0 and is never text: chevrons, the hollow waypoint's ring, a disabled control. The first draft had a 2.8:1 tertiary carrying half the cockpit's words.

Two warning hues, as the code has. --attn is ThemeRecipe.attentionInk, hue 65: the summons bar, the triangle, a held mark, a PR verdict that asks for you, the queue badge. --warn is warningInk, hue 95 at lower chroma, 30° off on purpose: a standing caution that is not a failure, which is rc off, a stale working-on age, and the context meter past its soft tier. The first draft collapsed them and was wrong to.

When the crew's hue is the summons's hue, in-progress marks go neutral. Ember (29), amber (68) and gold (95) sit within 40° of hue 65. For those crews --prog-mark and --prog-text resolve to the primary ink rather than the crew's accent, so the half-filled glyph, the current waypoint and the pull-request glyph cannot be mistaken for a held mark or a summons. The crew keeps its hue where it means identity: the rail dot, the selection wash, the canvas. The cockpit page shows Vivy Commercial selected.

What is genuinely broken today, as opposed to dated

  • The corrections queue hides its only high. The queue merges pr-open then open, newest first, and shows five rows (CockpitView.queueRowLimit). On 2026-09-04 the one severity: high item (the stale pinned rail row) is sixth, behind "18 more". Severity is decoded and never used to order. The mock sorts severity first, then recency.
  • Seven horizontal chrome rows in the centre column. Pane chrome (Local/Shell), working strip, status bar, conversation, live bar, composer, ribbon. Each is ~26pt with a hairline. The mock keeps every fact, every control and every slot (the status bar's text slot included) and draws them in three rows plus the ribbon.
  • Nothing collapses below 900pt. Rail fixed at 200, main pane floor 400, column floor 280, so the layout is identical at 900 and at 2000. The narrow page defines what gives way.
  • 10pt is the cockpit's body size. frameFont(.label) is base − 1 = 10pt, used for every epic title, queue row and proposal; DeckTypography's own comment calls 10pt "the floor" and reserves it for the send hint.
  • The off-screen rail fires only downward. It lists panels pushed below the viewport and is absent otherwise, so a reader scrolled to the queue has no non-scroll way back up. The cockpit page names what replacing it reverses.

What the mock does not change, and why

  • The ribbon is the last row and scrolls rather than shrinking its labels (the operator, 2026-08-25). The position at its right edge now reads "waypoint 4": it is the index of currentId, not a count, so it is not drawn as a fraction.
  • An epic's Done when: clamps at two lines with "more" as the escape hatch.
  • The rail's summons bar, the triangle, the accent selection wash, the trailing chevron and count, the "+ New" foot: kept. The rail page says why "+ New" stays for now.
  • The shell stamps the theme; the mocks show light and dark as two stamped frames, never as a media query.

Accessibility

Every element the redesign adds carries the label VoiceOver would read, in the HTML as aria-label and in the app as .accessibilityLabel, the way RowTreatment.accessibilityPrefix leads every row today because strike-through and glyph fill are invisible to it:

  • Queue count, orange: "Corrections queue, 23 items, 1 high". Severity chip: "severity high". Panel counts: "5 of 11 epics settled", "2 agents in flight".
  • Index strip entries: "Course, 5 of 11 epics settled. Activate to scroll to it." A collapsed panel's entry adds "collapsed".
  • Ribbon position: "waypoint 4 of 11"; the current waypoint: "Deck-to-Keel typed wire, work in progress".
  • START rows: "NYH Studio, not running; activate to start". A terminal-only row: "a terminal with no agent". A stopped crew: "Constance, stopped".
  • The off-screen summons chip: "1 session below needs an answer; activate to scroll to it".
  • Toolbar: the occupant control is a radio group named "Right column"; Terminal View is a toggle named "Terminal View"; it swaps the main pane and its way back is "Session View".

Buildability

Everything native here is SwiftUI: the toolbar is .toolbar with a Picker in .segmented style bound to ColumnOccupant and a separate toggle for showingTerminal; the index strip is a row of plain buttons driving ScrollViewProxy.scrollTo, with "here" read off the same PanelFramePreferenceKey frames the off-screen rail reads today (the top-most panel whose frame intersects the viewport); the dot rail is RailRow with its name hidden under a width threshold; the sheet is the existing occupant drawn as an overlay under the second breakpoint. The colours are the app's own solver at the app's own targets.
